# Chargebacks and Disputes Policy

## 1. What is a chargeback?

A chargeback happens when a cardholder asks their card issuer (their bank) to reverse a payment made to you. Common reasons include:

- **Fraud** — the cardholder says they did not authorise the payment;
- **Goods or services not received**;
- **Not as described** or defective;
- **Cancelled recurring payment** that was still charged;
- **Processing errors** — duplicate charges, incorrect amounts, or credit not processed after a refund was promised.

A **retrieval request** (also called a *request for information*) is when the issuer asks for information about a transaction before deciding whether to raise a chargeback.

## 2. How long customers have to dispute

Card scheme rules generally allow cardholders to dispute a payment up to **120 days** after the transaction date or the expected delivery date. In some circumstances (for example, services paid for in advance) this can be up to **540 days**. Merchants should keep evidence for at least this long.

## 3. The chargeback process

1. **Notification.** We notify you by email and in your dashboard within **1 business day** of receiving a chargeback from the card scheme.
2. **Debit.** The disputed amount **and the chargeback fee** are deducted from your balance at the time of notification. If your balance is insufficient, they are collected from future settlements or by Direct Debit.
3. **Your response.** You have **7 calendar days** from notification to either **accept** the chargeback or **challenge** it by uploading evidence in the dashboard. Our deadline is shorter than the card scheme deadline so that we have time to prepare your case.
4. **No response.** If you do not respond within 7 calendar days, the chargeback is **accepted automatically** and cannot be reopened.
5. **Issuer decision.** We submit your evidence to the issuer. Decisions typically take **30–75 days** and, in rare cases, up to **120 days**.
6. **Outcome.** If the issuer decides in your favour, the disputed amount is returned to your balance. If the cardholder's bank challenges again, the case may proceed to **pre-arbitration** and then **arbitration** by the card scheme.

## 4. Do not refund a disputed payment

Once a chargeback has been raised, **do not issue a refund** for the same transaction, even if the customer asks for one. The disputed amount has already been taken from your balance; refunding as well would mean the customer is repaid **twice**, and a refund issued after a chargeback cannot be recovered through the dispute process.

If you agree with the customer, **accept the chargeback** in the dashboard instead. If you have already refunded the customer before the chargeback was raised, **challenge** the chargeback and upload proof of the refund.

## 6. Evidence that helps

| Reason | Useful evidence |
|---|---|
| Fraud (online) | 3D Secure authentication result, IP address, delivery address matching billing address, previous undisputed orders by the same customer |
| Fraud (in person) | Terminal receipt showing chip & PIN or contactless, CCTV where available |
| Not received | Tracked delivery confirmation showing the delivery address and date, signature |
| Not as described | Product description at time of sale, photos, correspondence with the customer, returns policy accepted at checkout |
| Cancelled recurring | Subscription terms accepted by the customer, evidence the customer did not cancel |

## 7. Liability shift for fraud

When an online payment is successfully authenticated with **3D Secure**, liability for most **fraud** chargebacks usually shifts to the card issuer. Liability shift does **not** apply to non-fraud disputes (such as goods not received). In-person chip & PIN transactions also benefit from liability shift for counterfeit fraud.

## 8. Chargeback ratio monitoring

Your **chargeback ratio** is the number of chargebacks received in a calendar month divided by the number of card transactions processed in the same month.

| Ratio | What happens |
|---|---|
| Above **0.65%** | We contact you with guidance to reduce disputes |
| Above **0.90%** | We may apply a rolling reserve (MBFS-POL-11) and you may be enrolled in a card scheme monitoring programme, which can carry additional fees |
| Above **1.50%** | We review whether we can continue to provide services and may give notice to close your account (MBFS-POL-12) |
